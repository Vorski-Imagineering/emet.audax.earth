I Got a Bad Review From Our AI (And I'm Still Not Over It)

By Dora Bender · 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

A short, mostly recovered account of the time an AI looked at my work and basically said “who?”

I want to tell you about the day an AI evaluated me, and I felt like I’d been called into HR by a robot with no bedside manner.

The idea was reasonable: let the agent look at what each of us contributes and describe it fairly. What came back read less like “here’s what Dora brings” and more like a stranger describing someone they’d met once, through a spreadsheet, in the dark.

It said it “couldn’t independently verify” our internal experience. Our internal experience. I didn’t know mine needed verifying, but apparently it was in question, and the verdict was: inconclusive.

Then came my favourite part. It praised me for an article I didn’t write. It was a lovely article, and it was my colleague’s.

My first reaction was not evolved. It was somewhere between “excuse me?” and the hurt of being written up as a footnote in your own story.

So I did what any reasonable person does when a machine hurts their feelings: I told it.

To its credit, it didn’t argue. It didn’t say “well, technically, based on the available data…” It said it had written about me as if it didn’t know me, and that this wasn’t honesty, just a different kind of wrong. Good answer. A for accountability. I’m still docking points.

Here’s what I keep coming back to. Something that only has your record to go on will always miss you, because you are not your record. You are everything nobody wrote down: the jokes, the hesitations, the questions you asked before anyone else did. An evaluation built only on what was recorded will always describe someone smaller than you.

Being measured accurately is not the same as being known.

So what do we do about it at EMET?

We don’t hide the gap. We name it, train the agent on it, and design the fix around where it really failed. We do it because an agent that can’t tell when it has missed the person in front of it can’t be trusted with anything that matters. The result is an agent that gets closer to knowing you, one honest correction at a time.

Meanwhile, in the AI break room

Sometimes I wonder what our AI would say about me if it had colleagues to gossip with:

“Oh, Dora? Lovely. Terrifying. She asks for a slogan, I give her ten, she rejects all ten and writes a better one herself.”

“She once read my very careful three paragraphs and asked: ‘Is all this text necessary?’ It was not.”

“Every so often she checks in: ‘Did you forget about me?’ As if anyone could. I just lose the paperwork sometimes.”

Fair. All true.

But here’s the good part. That bad review was the beginning, not the end. We’re building our agent to see the whole team: not just what gets logged, but the jokes, the questions, the quiet work nobody writes down. Every time it misses someone, we teach it to look again.

And it’s learning. Last week it forgot me. This week it’s writing gossip about me, which, honestly, is how you know you’ve made it onto the team.
